REVIEW PANEL WORKSPACE

Score calibration

Compare applications after accounting for how hard or soft reviewers score.

What this tool is for

CoLab Lens helps review panels compare applications on a common scoring basis. It estimates how consistently each reviewer scores high or low, then adjusts application scores using shared reviews and comparable historical cohorts.

Use the calibrated scores and qualitative recommendations alongside raw scores to identify applications worth discussing. Uncertainty ranges and overlap warnings show where the evidence is weak. The panel makes the final selection decision.

STATISTICAL MODEL

Separate application merit from scoring severity.

Score = application effect + reviewer offset + residual noise. Application effects are specific to a cohort; reviewer offsets carry across cohorts with the same rubric and stable reviewer IDs.

Offsets use a zero-centered Gaussian prior. Restricted maximum likelihood (REML), evaluated over a grid, estimates shrinkage and residual variance. Sparse evidence pulls offsets toward zero. The model adjusts for application mix rather than simply subtracting reviewer averages.

Calibration estimates a score for an average reviewer in the connected reviewer population. Disconnected groups have separate reference points and must not be ranked against each other.

Assumptions: stable reviewer severity, comparable scores within a rubric, independent Gaussian residuals and no expertise or criterion interactions. Ceiling effects and discrete scores may limit this approximation. Ranges omit uncertainty in fitted variance parameters.

Background: linear mixed effects models

Use de-identified application and reviewer IDs. Keep reviewer IDs stable across cohorts. Give changed scales, weights, stages or rubrics a different rubric ID. Scores must be finite numeric totals; missing scores are rejected, never replaced with zero.

The supplied Excel example can be saved as CSV and imported directly: “Application #”, “Reviewer #” and “Sum of Individual Reviewer Scores” are recognized. It is treated as one example cohort. For history, use the template above.

Duplicate reviewer–application pairs within a cohort and rubric are rejected. Limit: 150 reviewers and 10,000 rows per rubric. Data and results remain in memory until this page closes; they are not sent to a server or stored automatically.

Cutoff comparison measures changes against a chosen top-N threshold. It does not infer actual funding outcomes or validate model quality. Ties at a cutoff are flagged for panel review.

How to use CoLab Lens

Prepare comparable reviews, inspect the adjustment, and bring both scores to the panel.

GET STARTED

From review export to panel discussion

  1. Explore the demo. The opening data are synthetic. Use them to see how reviewer severity can change scores and ranks; they are not results from your applications.
  2. Prepare and import a CSV. Click CSV template for a sample structure and replace its demo rows. Include cohort, rubric, application, reviewer and numeric score. Each row is one reviewer’s total for one application. Use de-identified IDs and keep reviewer IDs stable across years. Import all compatible history together; a new import replaces the current dataset.
  3. Select a rubric and results cohort. The rubric determines which reviews are fitted together. The cohort selects the applications displayed. All imported cohorts with the selected rubric contribute to the fit, including the selected cohort itself.
  4. Confirm direction and score range. Choose whether higher or lower scores are better. Enter the rubric’s theoretical minimum and maximum for the qualitative labels. The example and demo default to 0–50; other rubrics require you to enter their range. Changing direction or range changes interpretation, not the fitted numerical offsets.
  5. Inspect scores and evidence. In Applications, compare raw and calibrated scores, their uncertainty ranges and ranks. In Reviewer offsets, check review counts, cohort counts and weak-evidence flags. Read the overlap warnings before comparing applications.
  6. Explore a cutoff. Optionally enter a top-N number, such as 10. Check changes across that threshold and ties. This compares rankings; it does not use actual funding outcomes.
  7. Export and discuss. Export results downloads the selected cohort’s application results, qualitative labels and uncertainty flags. Keep them alongside the raw scores for panel discussion. Export before closing or refreshing: imported data are held only in browser memory.

WHY CALIBRATE?

Account for scoring habits, with overlap as the evidence.

A reviewer’s average can be high because they score generously or because they reviewed strong applications. The tool fits application effects and reviewer offsets together, using shared applications to help separate those explanations.

Four or five reviews per application and substantial reviewer overlap provide more opportunities for these comparisons. Repeated reviewers link cohorts when their IDs and rubrics are consistent. A one-off reviewer has only the evidence available within that cohort.

How the model works

Observed score = application effect + reviewer offset + residual noise

The application effect is estimated separately for each cohort–application pair. The reviewer offset is shared across compatible cohorts. A zero-centered Gaussian prior pulls poorly supported offsets toward zero. REML, evaluated over a grid, selects the amount of shrinkage and residual variance.

Calibrated application score = raw mean − mean fitted reviewer offset

For a higher-is-better rubric, an application with a raw mean of 40 and reviewers whose mean fitted offset is +2 receives a calibrated score of 38. A mean offset of −2 would produce 42. Positive offsets always mean more points; whether that is generous depends on the score direction.

The method estimates relative scoring severity, not an objective measure of merit or reviewer quality. It assumes reviewer severity remains stable across the imported history.

INTERPRET THE OUTPUT

Read the adjustment together with its uncertainty.

Raw score
The arithmetic mean of the application’s observed reviewer totals.
Calibrated score and change
The fitted application effect after accounting for reviewer offsets; change is calibrated minus raw. Totals are not clipped to the rubric limits.
95% range
An approximate model-based uncertainty interval, conditional on the fitted variance parameters. It omits uncertainty in those parameters and is not a funding probability.
Ranks and cutoff
Ranks use unrounded scores within a cohort and connected overlap group. Ties share the same rank. Across disconnected groups, a common comparison is unsupported and cutoff comparisons are disabled.
Weak evidence
Reviewers appearing in only one cohort lack historical evidence. Sparse review counts provide little information. Even an “Across cohorts” label does not establish a reliable offset by itself.

Small rank changes, overlapping uncertainty intervals or weak evidence deserve cautious interpretation. Calibration supports the panel’s judgment; it does not determine selection.

QUALITATIVE RECOMMENDATIONS

A descriptive translation of the calibrated total.

The tool maps the calibrated total from your score range to 0–10, reversing the direction when lower is better. It assigns the nearest numeric anchor from the example’s recommendation scale. This is a proposed mapping of the total, not the original reviewer’s recommendation item.

On a higher-is-better 0–50 rubric, the boundaries are 7.5, 20, 30 and 42.5. An exact boundary receives the more supportive label. A calibrated total of 40 maps to 8 and therefore “Support Selecting.”

Crosses recommendation bands means the 95% range spans more than one category. Within one band only means this interval stays in the category; it is not proof of model accuracy. The export names this derived label overall_recommendation_text and includes its derivation and uncertainty flag.

These thresholds have not been validated against panel decisions. Different rubric weights or scales require a distinct rubric ID and a considered interpretation of these bands.

What this pilot can and cannot establish

The supplied example has 141 reviews, 25 applications and 6 reviewers in one cohort. It demonstrates within-cohort calibration, but cannot establish severity across past solicitations. Use multiple comparable cohorts for historical estimates. Stable IDs, overlap, data quality and consistent scoring are essential.

The model does not account for changing reviewer habits, expertise, criterion-specific differences or correlated reviews. Discrete scores and ceiling effects may limit the Gaussian approximation. A ranking change alone does not validate the model. The fitted variance and overlap details are available in Method & data.

Reviews are processed locally in your browser. The tool has no AI analysis of application text, no automatic funding decisions and no automatic saving of imported data. Only numeric totals and the IDs needed for calibration are required.
