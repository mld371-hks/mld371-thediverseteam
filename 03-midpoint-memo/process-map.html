Caption

This maps the fourteen steps CIMIT currently runs to put a reviewer in front of a submitted proposal, read left to right within each phase band and then downward: intake and requirements, expert search and assessment, shortlist and outreach, the review itself, and the feedback that returns to the database. The shape worth noticing is the loop at the end — steps 12 to 14 take what was learned about a reviewer’s usefulness and return it to the record, which is what makes the next match better than the last. That loop is also the most fragile part: the meeting notes say roughly half of reviewers are one-off or business reviewers, so there is often no next time to learn into, and scores arrive uncalibrated because, as John put it, some reviewers are strict and some are kind. Steps 3, 6 and 9 are where information is lost and where the tool we are scoping would sit; steps 4 and 8 are deliberate human judgment worth protecting rather than automating.

What is still unconfirmed. Neither source records a single timing, so all thirteen hand-offs are marked ? delay rather than carrying invented numbers. Neither source marked steps as cost or signal either — the five colours here are drawn from the 1 October meeting notes and are our reading, not John’s words. The remaining nine steps are deliberately left unmarked rather than guessed at. Several roles and tools in the numbered account were already labelled “unconfirmed” at source; that wording is preserved rather than tidied away.

Not current state — proposed build, from the 1 October notes

Kept outside the flowchart on purpose. These are the features discussed, not steps CIMIT runs today.

  • Build a reviewer database, categorising reviewers by number rather than identifying information.
  • Rank reviewers on subjective measures and comments from prior reviewers.
  • Calibrate responses to correct for strict-versus-kind scoring variance.
  • Assemble an ideal panel — a mix of experience and score, SME with an average of 5 on a 1–10 scale.
  • Link a person to a score to a public document.
  • Next iteration: pull all reviewer scores out of a solicitation; use AI to gather bios; CIMIT supplies reviewer rankings and AI identifies the common characteristics of a good reviewer.
