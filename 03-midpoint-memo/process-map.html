Caption

This maps the seven steps CIMIT currently runs to put a reviewer panel in front of a submitted proposal, from the innovation team's submission through to a panel matched against CIMIT's stated ideal — subject-matter expertise plus an average reviewer score of 5 on a 1–10 scale. The process is sound at both ends and thin in the middle: the independence screen and the panel-matching criterion are deliberate quality checks worth protecting (gold), while expert sourcing, outreach, and scoring each lose information that CIMIT cannot get back (red) — expertise is pulled off LinkedIn with no reviewer database behind it, nobody records who was approached, and scores arrive uncalibrated because, as John put it, some reviewers are strict and some are kind. Roughly half the pool are one-off or business reviewers, so there is little repeat history to learn from. These three red steps are precisely where the tool we are scoping would sit.

Two gaps to close with John before this map is final. First, the meeting notes record no timings, so every hand-off is marked ? delay rather than carrying an invented number — these need to be filled in from him. Second, the notes never explicitly labelled steps as cost or signal; the red and gold marks here are our reading of what he described, and should be confirmed rather than assumed.

Not current state — proposed build, from the same notes

Kept outside the flowchart on purpose. These are the features discussed, not steps CIMIT runs today.

  • Build a reviewer database, categorising reviewers by number rather than identifying information.
  • Rank reviewers on subjective measures and comments from prior reviewers.
  • Calibrate responses to correct for strict-versus-kind scoring variance.
  • Assemble an ideal panel — a mix of experience and score, SME with an average of 5.
  • Link a person to a score to a public document.
  • Next iteration: pull all reviewer scores out of a solicitation; use AI to gather bios; CIMIT supplies reviewer rankings and AI identifies the common characteristics of a good reviewer.
